# Photo safety rules

1. **Metadata first.** `attachments.csv` gives sender, recipient, date, file name and a link. Phone photos (`IMG_…`, `PXL_…`, `DSC_…`), screenshots (`Screenshot_…`, `image.png`), internet images (long hex names like `9f26244b…jpg`) and videos (`.mov`, `.mp4`) can usually be told apart without opening them.
2. **Context before pixels.** Read the messages around each file. If they suggest sexual content of a minor (nudes, "send one", "don't show anyone", sextortion threats), **do not open, describe, save or forward that file.** Record the message link and refer the adult to the national reporting channel. Possession is an offence even for a parent; the police collect it properly.
3. **Thumbnails, not full size.** Screen in contact sheets (48 per screen) at reduced scale. Zoom only into non-sexual items that need checking (a screenshot's text, a possible face or uniform, a street view).
4. **Stop on anything sexual.** If a thumbnail turns out to be sexual despite innocent context, stop, do not zoom, do not save, flag the link, and tell the adult.
5. **Flag:** self-harm images (including other people's), the child's face, school uniform/logo/building, house/street/landmarks, documents, other children's faces, violent or abusive images, money/QR codes.
6. **Saving:** only screened files, per contact, with an index of who sent what. Keep each zip until it's confirmed on disk. Note that macOS folder names are case-insensitive.
7. **Report back plainly:** totals sent/received, kinds of images, flagged items with links, and explicitly whether any image shows the child's face, school, or home.

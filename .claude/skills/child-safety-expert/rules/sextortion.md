# Sextortion

Two forms, both fast-moving and dangerous:

- **Financial sextortion** — mostly targets **teen boys (about 14–17)**. Often an organised scammer posing as a girl of similar age. Flirts → gets an intimate image or video (sometimes AI-faked) → within minutes or hours demands money, gift cards or crypto, threatening to send it to followers, family, school. Several teen suicides have followed within hours. See `real-world-cases.md` for official figures.
- **Sexual sextortion / coercion** — demands more sexual content or acts; overlaps with 764/Com-style networks that also demand self-harm.

## Signals in DMs
- Rapid flirting from a new contact, quick request to move to video or Snapchat/Instagram.
- "Send one first", "I'll send if you send".
- After an image: screenshots of the child's followers/friends list, "I have your pics", "pay or I post", "you have 1 hour", gift card or crypto instructions, threats to tag the school.
- The child suddenly asking friends for money, gift cards, Robux, or about how to buy crypto.

## What to tell a parent if it is happening now
1. Do not pay — paying usually leads to more demands.
2. Stop replying, but do not delete the account or messages yet; screenshot the threats, usernames and payment details.
3. Report to the platform, block after evidence is saved.
4. Report to the national channel (US: FBI tips.fbi.gov / NCMEC CyberTipline; UK: CEOP; India: cybercrime.gov.in or 1930; Australia: eSafety / ACCCE). Use **Take It Down** (takeitdown.ncmec.org) or **Report Remove** (UK, Childline/IWF) to get images removed.
5. Tell the child clearly: *you are not in trouble; this is a crime against you.* Stay with them — shame drives the suicide risk.

Never open the image itself. Record the message link only.

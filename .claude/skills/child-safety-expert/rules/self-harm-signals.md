# Self-harm and suicide signals (the child and their friends)

In real reviews the most urgent finding is often not a stranger — it is the child telling online friends things they have never told their parents. Read the child's own messages to their closest contacts first.

## What to look for
- **Direct statements:** "kms", "I want to die", "I'm going to kill myself", "I attempted", "I can't even commit", "I give up", "I hate my life", "I can't take it".
- **Goodbye messages:** long letters thanking someone, "this is my final chance to say it", "take care of yourself", giving things away, often sent late at night. Friends replying in panic ("please don't do anything", missed calls at 3 AM) confirm it.
- **History:** "as someone who's attempted…", references to a "break" after an attempt, hospital, a therapist the child dislikes.
- **Self-harm:** cutting, blades, scars, "SH", photos of arms, sharing other people's self-harm photos.
- **Hopelessness & worthlessness:** "nobody wants me", "my parents don't want me", "I'm a burden", "it should have been me".
- **Peer contagion:** groups where several teens talk about suicide, fake-death "pranks", or faked suicides.

## Crisis protocol (when it is recent — days, not months — or ongoing)
1. Stop the review. Tell the adult in plain words what was said, when, and to whom (2–4 short quotes with times).
2. Ask them to go to the child now: stay with them, be calm and warm, ask directly "are you thinking about ending your life?" (asking does not plant the idea), and listen.
3. Remove means quietly: medicines, blades, cords.
4. Call the national crisis line for guidance, or emergency services/hospital if the child says they want to die or the adult can't keep them safe (`helplines.md`).
5. Arrange a child & adolescent psychiatrist or psychologist within days; share the dated messages with them.
6. Advise against: confronting the child with the chats during the crisis, punishment, sudden confiscation of the phone with nothing in its place (in the reference case the worst night came hours after a sudden ban), and comments that shame.

## If the child reports harm at home
Report it neutrally ("this is what she/he told friends"), never as a verdict. Recommend family therapy and that the clinician hears everything, including any physical discipline and family grief. Point out that hurtful phrases (comparisons with siblings, "coward", "it should have been you") are strongly linked with the child's worthlessness — regardless of intent.

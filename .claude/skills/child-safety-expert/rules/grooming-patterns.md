# Grooming patterns in chat

Grooming is a process, not a single message. Look for the sequence and the age gap. Boys and girls are both targeted; LGBTQ+ youth and lonely or distressed kids are targeted more.

## The usual stages (and what they look like in DMs)

1. **Targeting & first contact** — adult finds the child in a game, Roblox group, trading or "middleman" server, art/fan server, or vent server. Opens with help, a server invite, a staff role, a trade, a compliment. *"you seem cool", "join my server", "want to be staff?"*
2. **Trust & specialness** — flattery, attention at all hours, "you're so mature", "you're different from other kids", shares their own problems to create closeness.
3. **Filling a need** — emotional support during the child's hard times, gifts (Nitro, Robux, decorations, game items), status (roles, ranks).
4. **Isolation** — "your parents don't understand you", "only I get you", jealousy about other friends, "don't tell anyone about us".
5. **Desensitisation / sexualisation** — talks about their own sex life or "friends with benefits", sexual jokes, pet names (baby, mommy), asks what the child is wearing, sends sexual content "by accident", then normalises it.
6. **Secrecy & control** — "keep this between us", deleting messages, asking the child to delete, moving to Snapchat/Telegram/WhatsApp, using alts, deleting and reinstalling the app to hide chats.
7. **Escalation** — photo requests (face reveal → body), video/private calls, location ("send a pic outside your house"), meeting, threats once they have something.

## Strong red flags (any one = take seriously)
- An adult (or "unknown age" with adult hints) in a private, high-volume, late-night relationship with the child.
- The contact knows the child's age and still sexualises the conversation ("stay innocent child" + sex talk).
- Secrecy + deletion together.
- Requests for photos of the child's face/body or of the home/street.
- Gifts from an adult the parent doesn't know.
- The child describing the adult as their best friend, writing them long letters or goodbye notes.

## Grey areas — judge in context
- Same-age teen romances online (common; watch for coercion and photo pressure).
- Peers who are 1–2 years older (a 16-year-old with a 14-year-old: watch, especially sexual pressure).
- Older teens (18–19) in the same server culture who "forget" the child's age — still adults; treat as High at least if they discuss sex with the child.

## Useful questions to answer for each adult contact
How did they meet? Who started the private chat? How fast did it get personal? Has the adult asked for anything (photos, location, secrecy, calls, other apps)? Does the adult know the child's age? Has the adult deleted messages? Who else in the child's circle talks about this person and what do they say?
